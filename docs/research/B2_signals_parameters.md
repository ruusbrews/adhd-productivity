# Evidence Base for an Adaptive Digital ADHD-Support Simulator: Signals, Parameters, Reward Design, Harms and Existing Tools

The most important finding is that only two signals have strong, consistent ADHD-versus-neurotypical evidence: reaction-time variability (a behavioural signal) and actigraphy-measured movement. Pupillometry, oculomotor inhibition and task-related HRV have moderate or mixed support. Blink rate, EEG theta/beta ratio, resting HRV and keystroke dynamics have weak, null or no ADHD-specific evidence. None of the eight signals has been validated as a real-time trigger for adaptive ADHD intervention, so the simulator should treat every physiological-to-attention mapping as a tunable, uncertain assumption.

## TL;DR
- **Signals:** Use RT variability and movement as the primary simulated markers of ADHD state. Model HRV, EDA and pupil size as arousal signals that confound attention with emotion, and model theta/beta ratio as a null or weak marker. No signal is validated for real-time ADHD intervention.
- **Parameters and reward:** Published effect sizes, not raw rates, are the best simulator anchors. RT variability g = 0.76 in children and 0.46 in adults; overall CPT deficit is large but the time-on-task decline is only small-to-moderate; in the HeartSteps trial (Klasnja et al., Ann Behav Med 2019) the prompt effect "diminished linearly over time at 2% per day." ADHD users prefer immediate, frequent reinforcement but carry elevated risk of problematic gaming (r = 0.296 with ADHD symptom severity, Koncz et al. 2023) and impulsive buying, so variable-ratio or monetised mechanics should be treated as a harm parameter.
- **Harms and tools:** Notifications measurably raise inattention and hyperactivity symptoms even in non-ADHD samples. Stigma from "you are distracted" alerts is argued in peer-reviewed critique but has not been measured empirically. The only FDA-authorised ADHD *treatments* found (EndeavorRx; Monarch eTNS) use no physiological sensing for adaptation, and sensing-based products (QbTest, NEBA) are cleared only as *assessment aids*.

## Key Findings

1. **Behavioural signals beat physiological ones.** Kofler et al.'s meta-analysis of 319 studies (Clin Psychol Rev 2013) found that RT variability is reliably elevated in ADHD. It is also not specific to ADHD: adolescents and adults with ADHD were indistinguishable from clinical controls.\[1\] Actigraphy shows robust excess movement, but this depends on context.\[2\]
2. **The attention/emotion boundary is genuinely blurred for autonomic signals.** HRV, EDA and pupil size are interpreted in the literature as arousal or regulation indices. The same signals are used both in sustained-attention studies and in emotion-induction studies of ADHD.\[3\] A simulator that infers "inattention" from them is making an untested leap, which justifies strict regulation of that inference.
3. **The theta/beta ratio should be modelled as a null marker.** Arns, Conners & Kraemer's meta-analysis (J Atten Disord 2013) concluded that "Excessive TBR cannot be considered a reliable diagnostic measure of ADHD," and Boxum et al.'s subtyping meta-analysis (Appl Psychophysiol Biofeedback 2025) "confirms previous findings suggesting that TBR has no diagnostic value for ADHD."
4. **Engagement decays fast.** In the HeartSteps trial (Klasnja et al., Ann Behav Med 2019), the walking-suggestion effect "diminished linearly over time at 2% per day (e^b = .98, p < .01)" and "was no longer distinguishable from zero by the 28th day." Response to survey-style prompts falls from roughly 89% to 70% over 14 weeks.\[4\]
5. **Regulatory precedent is narrow.** EndeavorRx's FDA indication is limited to a computer-measured attention outcome and explicitly warns it "may not display benefits in typical behavioral symptoms, such as hyperactivity."

**Interpretation for inference regulation.** Only eye-movement inhibition and RT variability are read mainly as *attention/cognitive control*. HRV, EDA and pupil size are read as *arousal*, and they are used in both attention and emotion paradigms. Actigraphy, blink and keystroke signals carry *physical/fatigue* variance. I found no study that experimentally separated attention from emotion using the same signal in ADHD. The closest evidence is that EDA is used both for sustained-attention hypo-arousal and for emotion-induction reactivity in ADHD samples.\[3\]\[9\] The simulator should therefore label any autonomic-derived inference as "arousal (construct-ambiguous)" rather than "inattention."

## Part 3 — Reward Schedules and Persuasive Design

- **Immediate, high-intensity, frequent reinforcement helps; delay hurts.** Luman, Oosterlaan & Sergeant reviewed 22 studies (N = 1,181 children). They found reinforcement improves performance in both groups, the effect is "somewhat more prominent in AD/HD," "high intensity of reinforcement is highly effective," and children with ADHD "prefer immediate over delayed reward." Psychophysiologically, however, ADHD children "seem less sensitive to reinforcement."\[47\]
- **Fixed/continuous vs. variable/partial schedules.** In an experimental reinforcement-learning study, all children acquired behaviour faster under continuous than partial reinforcement. "Stretching the ratios" (continuous fading to partial) sped acquisition while preserving persistence. After partial reinforcement, though, children with ADHD made fewer target responses during extinction than TD children.\[48\]\[49\] Luman et al. (2008) found ADHD decision-making was "sensitive to frequency but blind to the magnitude of penalty."\[50\] **Design implication:** start dense and predictable, thin gradually, and avoid unpredictable penalties.
- **Delay aversion.** A comparative meta-analysis (Marx et al. 2021) attributes impulsive choice in ADHD to "stronger than normal aversion toward delay" interacting with the demotivating effect of hypothetical rewards.\[51\] An EEG study found adolescents with ADHD showed larger anticipatory CNV to cues where delay could be escaped, supporting delay escape as reinforcing.\[52\] Streak timers or "wait to unlock" mechanics are therefore likely to be aversive rather than motivating.
- **Streaks/gamification.** I found **no peer-reviewed ADHD-specific trial of streaks** in the time available. This is an evidence gap. The relevant precedent is that EndeavorRx "leverages art, music, storytelling, and reward cycles" but enforces "lock-out after the allocated gameplay," a built-in cap on compulsive engagement.\[53\]
- **Compulsive engagement risk.** Koncz et al.'s meta-analysis (Clin Psychol Rev 2023) found "a moderate relationship between GD and ADHD symptom severity" (r = 0.296; inattention r = 0.306, hyperactivity r = 0.266), with higher gaming-disorder severity in ADHD than non-ADHD groups (g = 0.693); a separate meta-analysis links ADHD and internet addiction (Wang et al., BMC Psychiatry 2017). Both are cross-sectional, so causality is unknown.
- **Overspending risk.** Adults with ADHD (n = 225) scored higher on the Buying Impulsiveness Scale than students (n = 121): mean **30.78 vs 19.57**. The link was mediated by ability to defer gratification.\[54\]\[55\]\[56\] **Contradictory:** in a community sample of 1,292, ADHD symptoms were no longer associated with impulsive buying after controlling for personality, depression and demographics.\[57\] Treat any in-app purchase or loot-box-style variable reward as a high-risk parameter for ADHD agents.

## Part 4 — Notification and Intervention Harms

- **Interruption cost.** Stothart et al. (J Exp Psychol Hum Percept Perform 2015) found that "cellular phone notifications alone significantly disrupted performance on an attention-demanding task, even when participants did not directly interact with a mobile device."\[58\] The lead author's summary adds that the effects were comparable to those reported for actively texting or talking.\[59\]
- **Over-alerting raises ADHD-like symptoms.** In a two-week within-subjects experiment (Kushlev, Proulx & Dunn, CHI 2016; 221 undergraduates, not selected for ADHD), "participants reported higher levels of inattention and hyperactivity when alerts were on."\[60\]\[61\] Inattention was 2.38 vs 2.27 (d = .44) and hyperactivity 2.11 vs 2.01 (d = .45) on 4-point scales. These are self-reports; the raw differences are about 0.1 points. There was no direct effect on productivity or well-being.\[62\]
- **Habituation.** Prompt effects decay (HeartSteps, Klasnja et al. 2019: "diminished linearly over time at 2% per day," null by day 28), and the effect was weaker when more suggestions had been sent recently. Over-alerting thus both harms attention and erodes intervention value.
- **Anxiety from false "you are distracted" feedback and stigma.** **No empirical study was found** that measures anxiety or stigma from false-positive distraction alerts in ADHD users; this is an evidence gap, not a negative finding. The strongest peer-reviewed source is a critical review by neurodivergent researchers (Spiel et al., CHI 2022). It discusses a system that fitted children with 8 accelerometers, a heart-rate sensor and an EEG headset to alert lost attention in class: "In addition to the obvious issue of stigma, there is no consideration of the fact that such alerts might feasibly act as distractors in and of themselves, and may well cause distress to the child." The review concludes that ADHD technologies "are largely used to 'mitigate' the experiences of ADHD which are perceived as disruptive to neurotypical standards of behaviour."\[63\] A small qualitative CHI 2026 study (6 autistic and/or ADHD participants; 3 self-tracked) found "self-tracking imposes substantial interpretive and emotional demands," with anxiety that became validating when shared.\[64\]
- **Opposite risk.** Kushlev et al. also note that turning alerts off can cause anxiety about missing notifications, so "fewer alerts" is not cost-free.\[62\]

## Recommendations for the Simulator

1. **Primary state variables:** RT variability (presentation-scaled using Kofler 2013 g-values) and load-dependent motion (Kofler 2016). Make the vigilance offset large and the decay slope only modestly steeper than NT (Huang-Pollock 2012).
2. **Physiological channels:** Implement HRV, EDA and pupil size as a latent **arousal** variable with an explicit emotion component. Block any rule mapping them directly to "inattention" without a behavioural corroborator. Include TBR only as a null-effect control.
3. **Prompt policy:** Add habituation (2%/day decay as a default, per Klasnja et al. 2019's e^b = .98; sensitivity range up to steeper for ADHD agents), a dose-dependent penalty for recent prompt count, and a notification-induced symptom increase of roughly d ≈ 0.4.
4. **Reward module:** Use continuous-then-thinned schedules and avoid delay-gated rewards. Model monetised or variable-ratio rewards as a harm pathway, with elevated risk weights for impulsive presentations. Cap session engagement, following the EndeavorRx lock-out precedent.
5. **Harm metrics:** Log false-positive "distracted" alerts as a cost even though the magnitude is empirically unknown. Report results under several assumed stigma/anxiety costs.

## Caveats

- Several numbers come from search-result abstracts or institutional records rather than full texts, notably Arns 2013, Huang-Pollock 2012, Luman 2005 and Koncz 2023. HeartSteps figures were confirmed via the abstract text, not the full article.
- Notification and habituation parameters come from **non-ADHD** samples.
- Items marked verified: false (QbCheck K190726, Monarch CE mark, the 30 vs 23 blinks/min figure) should be checked before use. EndeavorOTC's sample size conflicts between sources (221 in Akili's SEC 8-K vs 223 on a secondary site), as does its clearance date (18 vs 14 June 2024).
- Presentation-specific (inattentive / hyperactive-impulsive / combined) parameters are largely **not reported** in the literature. Splits in this report are construct-based assumptions, not pooled estimates.
- This is a research simulation synthesis and makes no clinical claims.
