## Provisions relevant to a digital ADHD-support app (paraphrased)

### Art. 5(1)(f) examples [verified]
An education institution using emotion recognition to infer students' interest and attention is a prohibited example (para. 255); gaze tracking that only records fixation points without inferring emotions is not prohibited (para. 255); fatigue detection for pilots/drivers is not emotion recognition (para. 249, 261). The medical exception is narrow: therapeutic use means CE-marked medical devices, general wellbeing monitoring is not covered (para. 256-257), while assisting students with autism can qualify (para. 263).
Relevance: Directly maps onto attention-state monitoring; avoid institutional deployment. A wellness-claim app cannot rely on the medical exception.

### Intentions; biometric limitation [verified]
Covers inference of emotions and intentions, but only on the basis of biometric data (para. 244-245, 250). Biometric data includes behavioural biometrics such as keystrokes (way of typing), eye tracking and heartbeats (para. 251); inferring emotions from written text is outside (para. 251, 265).
Relevance: Gaze, HRV/EDA and keystroke dynamics are biometric; typed content sentiment is not. The guidelines do not say whether task-performance data (missed items, reaction-time variability) counts as behavioural biometrics.
