## Provisions relevant to a digital ADHD-support app (paraphrased)

### Art. 5(1)(f) [verified]
Bans placing on the market, putting into service or using AI to infer emotions of people in workplace and education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons.
Relevance: School/employer deployment inferring attention or frustration from biometrics is likely prohibited unless medical; consumer self-use is outside.

### Art. 5(1)(a)-(b) [verified]
Bans manipulative/deceptive techniques and exploitation of vulnerabilities due to age, disability or socio-economic situation that materially distort behaviour and cause significant harm.
Relevance: Reward loops or nudges exploiting impulsivity (e.g., to drive purchases) risk this ban. Commission guidelines C(2025) 884 para. 105 give as a prohibited example a game that gives children personalised, unpredictable rewards through addictive reinforcement schedules, where it causes significant harm.

### Art. 3(39); Recital 18 [verified]
Emotion recognition means inferring emotions or intentions from biometric data; physical states such as pain or fatigue are excluded.
Relevance: Whether 'attention' is an emotion or a cognitive/physical state decides applicability. Commission guidelines para. 240 and 255 treat inferring learners' attention or interest as emotion recognition; fatigue stays excluded (para. 249, 261).

### Art. 6, Annex III (1(c), 3); Arts. 13-14 [UNVERIFIED]
Certain emotion recognition and education AI is high-risk, requiring transparency and effective human oversight including override.
Relevance: Education or emotion-recognition variants become high-risk from 2 Dec 2027 (post-Omnibus).

### Art. 50(3) [UNVERIFIED]
Deployers of emotion recognition systems must inform exposed persons.
Relevance: Tell users when physiological signals are used to infer affective states.
